About the Workshop

Bridging perception, simulation, and learning within the emerging paradigm of surgical digital twins.

This workshop brings together researchers across robotics, computer-assisted intervention, and machine learning to examine emerging directions in digital twins for surgical robotics. The primary objective is to explore how perception, simulation, and learning can be integrated within a unified digital twin framework to support robotic perception, planning, control, and intraoperative decision-making.

The workshop will feature invited talks, peer-reviewed contributed presentations, and moderated panel discussions centered on open challenges such as real-time deployment, multimodal fusion, physics-based modeling and simulation, generalization across procedures, benchmarking, and safety-critical validation.

Workshop Themes

The technical program is organized around three complementary themes reflecting the state of the art and key open challenges.

Theme 01

Perception & Representation Learning

Multimodal perception from surgical video, robotic kinematics, force sensing, and imaging. Structured representation learning, scene understanding, and modeling of tool–tissue interactions.

Theme 02

Simulation & Digital Twin Modeling

Physics-aware modeling of deformable anatomy, data-driven and differentiable simulation, hybrid and reduced-order formulations, uncertainty modeling, and real-time perception–simulation–control loops.

Theme 03

Learning & Generative Methods

Generative models and world models for prediction, planning, and data augmentation. Closed-loop robot learning, sim-to-real transfer, and data-efficient learning strategies for surgical robotics.

Endoluminal Robotics and Embodied AI: Bridging Perception and Intelligence for Surgical Digital Twins

Large Foundation Model Empowered Surgical Intelligence

With global surgical volumes estimated by the Lancet Commission to exceed 300 million procedures annually, the need for advances in procedural safety and efficacy is more critical than ever. While minimally invasive techniques, facilitated by high-resolution laparoscopy, have demonstrably improved patient recovery profiles and intraoperative visualization, the inherent complexity of human anatomy demands a new paradigm. Artificial Intelligence (AI) is now fundamentally benefiting surgical care, with the emergence of the “AI copilot” representing a pivotal innovation. In this talk, I will present our latest interdisciplinary research at AI for various perioperative tasks, to support surgical education, intra-operative navigation and even semi-autonomous surgery, via improved surgical scene understanding, reasoning and anticipation. Simulation Tools in SOFA for Soft Surgical Robotics

SOFA is an open-source simulation framework originally developed for surgical simulation, with a particular focus on the modeling of soft tissues. Over the years, it has also become widely used for the simulation of soft robots, making it a particularly relevant platform for the development of soft robotic technologies for surgical applications. This presentation will introduce several simulation tools available in SOFA, including hyper- and visco-elastic finite element modeling, contact and interaction modeling, and inverse modeling methods. Then, through concrete examples, we will illustrate how these tools can support different stages of soft surgical robot development, from robot design and optimization to control and autonomous navigation. We will also discuss how SOFA-based simulation could contribute to bridging soft robotics and artificial intelligence.

What Can a Surgical Scene Become? Generalized Digital Twins as the Enabling Layer in Surgical Robotics

Surgical data being rare is a problem everybody can relate to, but even when surgical data is abundant, the problems do not stop: it is unstructured, correlated, and of course, unlabeled. In this talk, I will present our recent progress on solving these notorious challenges. Leveraging our foundational work on generalized digital twins — a structured, adaptable representation that consolidates spatial and temporal information — we turn raw surgical data into machine learning-ready assets and show how these representations further enable complex downstream tasks, including counterfactual generation and generalizable inference.

Modularity, Intermediate Representations, and Small Models to Structure Learned Surgical Autonomy

In the surgical domain, where internet-scale data either does not exist or does not have high fidelity, end-to-end learning and generalist models are not naturally poised to succeed. Additionally, the safety constraints of surgery require consistent, verifiable, and inspectable autonomy. In this talk I’ll lay out my case for the use of small, modular models which leverage intermediate representations and/or explicit probabilistic reasoning in surgical autonomy. These paradigms enable the integration of established engineering methodology with the performance of data-driven methods, a critical component in translating these concepts into regulated, safety-critical domains.

Real-time Capable Learning-based Visual Tool Pose Correction via Differentiable Digital Twin
Shuyuan Yang, Zonghe Chua
Abstract

Autonomy in robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery has the potential to reduce surgeon cognitive and task load, thereby increasing procedural efficiency. However, implementing accurate autonomous control can be difficult due to poor end-effector proprioception. Joint encoder readings are typically inaccurate due to kinematic non-idealities in their cable-driven transmissions. Vision-based pose estimation approaches are highly effective, but lack real-time capability, generalizability, or can be hard to train. In this work, we demonstrate a real-time capable, Vision Transformer-based pose estimation approach that is trained using end-to-end differentiable kinematics and rendering. We demonstrate the potential of this approach to correct for noisy pose estimates through a real robot dataset and the potential real-time processing ability. Our approach is able to reduce more than 50\% of hand-eye translation errors in the dataset, reaching the same performance level as an existing optimization-based method. Our approach is four times faster, and capable of near real-time inference at 22 Hz. A zero-shot prediction on an unseen dataset shows good generalization ability, and can be further finetuned for increased performance without human labeling.

2
Geometry-First Generative Worlds as Simulation Substrates in Bronchoscopy
Chris von Csefalvay, Pranav Doma
Abstract

The limiting factor for endoluminal robotics in bronchoscopic approaches is not algorithms but supervision -- essentially, a data problem: recordings are scarce, privacy-encumbered and rarely carry ground truth for pose nor depth. We describe SynAirG, a manifest-driven procedural pipeline that turns chest CTs into navigable, metric bronchoscopy worlds. In the generative setting, procedurally synthesised volumes are reduced to quality-assured airway meshes and centreline graphs, procedure-style scope trajectories are sampled, and frame-aligned depth, surface normals and per-pixel shading streams are rendered with exact pose. All of this is packaged in the LeRobot and TUM trajectory formats, ready for SLAM training or other downstream applications. We validate the methodology on a large set of synthetic cases, characterising anatomical and kinematic spread. We then report pitfalls with equal candour, including how corpus-level fingerprinting of the full release exposed silent anatomical mode collapse caused by cache reuse upstream of otherwise-passing per-case quality gates, and we outline two unaddressed realism gaps, conditional neural appearance for mucosal pathology and prescribed respiratory deformation, with concrete pathways for both. Geometry-first generation reconciles the desire for realism with the need for customisability and cross-temporal stability.

FEM Paradigms for Heterogeneity Modeling in Multi-Material Tissue-Tumor Surgical Twins
Abhinaba Bhattacharjee, Noah Shaikh, Dimitrios Stefanidis, Ahmed H. Qureshi, Sohel Anwar
Abstract

Preoperative Robotic Laparoscopy rehearsals on patient-specific digital twins must predict organ deformation from applied loads and peripheral tissue interactions alone during interactive simulations. Subsurface tumors are invisible to the endoscopic camera, so interpenetration or drift between internal layers cannot be corrected by registration from video-learned surrogates as the simulation advances. Therefore, biomechanical modeling of interactive tissue-tumor digital twins is challenging, as interlayer displacement continuity requires Finite Element Method (FEM) formulations. We evaluate three FEM paradigms on a single Computed Tomography derived ex vivo liver scene, a) uncoupled and b) constraint-coupled multi-body liver-tumor formulations, and c) a single multi-material volumetric mesh partitioned into Region-of-Interest (ROI) derived multi-elastic subdomains requiring no constraints. Both multi-body formulations lose tissue interface continuity under gravitational settling with Tumor separation from parenchyma, whereas the single-mesh multi-material paradigm preserves continuity by construction.We compare tumor palpation of da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) patient side manipulator (PSM) tools on the Multimaterial FEM model with varying tumor elasticities against a tumor-free baseline. We report a tumor-to-parenchyma stiffness contrast of 1.24 to 1.30 from Lagrange Multiplier recovered contact forces, tradeoff between constraint correction fidelity, and interactive frame rates.

Federated Bayesian Surveillance of Mechanical Thrombectomy Adverse Events: A Population Risk Layer for Surgical Digital Twins
Damini Rijhwani
Abstract

Learned surgical simulators and world models can roll out plausible procedural futures, but they carry no grounded estimate of how often interventional devices actually harm patients. We propose treating population-scale adverse-event surveillance as a distinct belief layer of the surgical digital twin, and we evaluate a federated Bayesian protocol for learning it under formal privacy guarantees. Each site holds per-class Gamma-Poisson posteriors over adverse-event rates and exchanges only Rényi-differentially-private natural-parameter updates. We benchmark on the complete FDA MAUDE cohort for thrombus-retrieval catheters (product code NRY): 8,617 reports, of which 6,491 are classified by transparent keyword rules into five thrombectomy complication classes and partitioned across $K{=}8$ manufacturer sites. At a matched privacy budget of $(\varepsilon{\approx}2.09, \delta{=}10^{-5})$, the conjugate protocol attains a held-out Poisson score of $-5.78$ per test event versus $-26.58$ for FedAvg with differential privacy. The non-private federated model also outperforms centralized pooling ($+3.19$ vs $+2.93$), evidence that manufacturer-specific complication profiles are real and that federation preserves them. Because MAUDE lacks procedure denominators, outputs are relative rate orderings rather than absolute risks, and we report all privacy-utility operating points.

A Belief-State World Model for Catheter Navigation under Sparse Fluoroscopy: A Planar Proof of Concept
Damini Rijhwani
Abstract

Endovascular catheter navigation relies on continuous fluoroscopy, exposing patients and clinical staff to ionizing radiation throughout the procedure. We investigate whether a physics-based world model can sustain the navigation task between deliberately sparse X-ray acquisitions, and whether the model can signal when its internal state estimate is no longer reliable. We formulate sparse-fluoroscopy navigation as a partially observable Markov decision process where a Cosserat rod simulator supplies transition dynamics, sparse noisy projections provide observations, and a particle filter maintains a belief over the device state, reduced in this implementation to a tip state with a geometric contact proxy. We evaluate this formulation in a deliberately simplified setting: a synthetic planar vessel phantom with a quasi-static rod model and simulated projections, without clinical or animal data. In this setting, the belief-state model tracks the simulated tip with a root mean square error of 1.30 mm while acquiring observations at one fifteenth of the continuous rate (0.97 mm at every frame, 1.51 mm at one thirtieth), reported 90 percent credible intervals achieve 0.92 empirical coverage, and the belief-derived contact risk estimate discriminates unsafe contact events with an AUROC of 0.68. These results constitute a proof of concept on a simplified simulation rather than a demonstration of clinical readiness; their purpose is to establish that calibrated belief, rather than point-estimate accuracy alone, is the essential property a sparse-imaging world model must deliver.

Flexible Sensors for Shape Sensing and Tissue Analysis
Kaiyi Huang, Zihao Li, Anthony Riello, Vincent G. Capone, Sanzhar Malik
Abstract

This report presents a preliminary investigation into utilizing piezoresistive Carbon Nanotube (CNT) and Flexible Printed Circuit Board (FPCB) sensors for shape sensing and biological tissue analysis. To address the inherent non-linearities of CNT sensors, an initial calibration procedure was developed, and hysteresis compensation was discussed as a key requirement for future improvement. Furthermore, multi-layer tissue phantoms mimicking human skin were fabricated to evaluate the system's response during syringe needle insertion. While the preliminary tensile calibration and experimental setup design demonstrate the basic feasibility of this approach, further curvature tracking and pressure profiling experiments are still required for more complete validation. In parallel, a custom multi-channel FPCB impedance sensor was designed to differentiate between biological materials such as blood plasma, vessel walls, and clot material. Utilizing handmade single channel prototypes, preliminary testing demonstrated that by employing 2 ms pulsed DC voltages and a differential impedance ($\Delta Z$) tracking algorithm, the system successfully compensates for the parallel conductivity of surrounding fluids. These initial findings provide crucial insights into the physical and algorithmic constraints of the setup, establishing a fundamental baseline and clearly defining the necessary hardware optimizations and advanced signal processing techniques required for future work.

Real-Time Whole-Body Contact Estimation of Continuum Robots Using Multiplexed Fibers for Embodied Actuation and Sensing to Quantify Interactions
Zecai Lin, Jingyuan Xia, Zheng Xu, Yun Zou, Cheng Zhou, Jiafan Chen, Lucas Tat-Long Tong, Shaoping Huang, Huanghua Liu, Weidong Chen, Guang-Zhong Yang, Anzhu Gao
Abstract

Millimeter cable-driven continuum robots exhibit shape conforming, dexterous manipulation capabilities in constrained environments. They are increasingly used for narrow space and endoluminal intervention. For delicate manipulation, quantifying force interaction between the robot and its surrounding environment is important for both shape adjustment and avoiding damages to luminal structures. In this work, we propose a real-time, whole-body contact estimation framework for small-scale continuum robots, based on actuation fibers and model-informed neural networks. The physical relationship among external body contact, internal actuation and shape sensing of the continuum robot is formulated based on rod theory, and the body contact estimation is treated as an inverse problem given the actuation tension profile and robot shape as inputs. The contact position and force are estimated using a neural network, and a generative adversarial network (GAN)-based data augmentation strategy is proposed to reduce the need for large amounts of real data from the continuum robot under external forces. Additionally, an automatic data acquisition platform is developed to efficiently collect the small amount of required data. Experiments with notched continuum robots were conducted to demonstrate the general applicability and accuracy of the proposed approach. The results show that the mean estimation errors for the 3D contact position and contact force magnitude are 1.7 mm (2.3%) and 8.7 mN (5.8%), respectively, with an estimation frequency of 25 Hz. It paves the way for embodied integration using multiplexed fibers for the simultaneous actuation and sensing of millimeter-scale continuum robots, enabling safer operation in confined spaces through machine intelligence.

Human Interface Device-Driven Teleoperation of a Robot-Assisted Ureteroscopy Platform and its Digital Twin for Laser Lithotripsy
Sarah Schürer, Sarvesh Saini, Julio Ojalvo, Jonathan E. Katz, Ubbo Visser
Abstract

We present a three-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) robot-assisted ureteroscopy platform that integrates ureteroscope motion and laser lithotripsy control through a single human interface device (HID). A Wolf RIWO digital ureteroscope is actuated in linear translation, axial rotation, and distal-tip flexion using two stepper motors and one servo motor controlled through a Teensy 4.1 microcontroller. During teleoperation, joystick deflection proportionally commands motion speed in all three degrees of freedom, providing continuous proportional control. Position sensing supports axis monitoring, software limits, and homing, while the ST3020 flexion servo additionally incorporates internal magnetic position sensing and local closed-loop servo control. A Dornier MedTech lithotripsy laser is integrated through relay-based ready and fire interfaces adapted from the laser footswitch connection, with a software interlock requiring Enable and Ready states before Fire can be activated. Software-defined dead zones, axis gating, velocity limits, and homing functions further reduce unintended commands. In an initial benchtop demonstration, a novice operator under guidance successfully targeted and fired on eight stone targets using the integrated controller. The cumulative movement-to-fire time was 5 min 48 s, corresponding to a mean of 43.5 s per target, excluding laser firing duration and pauses between targets. This work demonstrates the feasibility of combining ureteroscope manipulation and laser control within a unified teleoperation interface.

Ready to Fire: A Novel DT–Enhanced Vision-Based Navigation Framework for Autonomous Robotic-Assisted Ureteroscopy
Julio Ojalvo, Sarvesh Saini, Jonathan E. Katz, Ubbo Visser
Abstract

Robust kidney stone segmentation is a critical capability for robotic-assisted ureteroscopy (RA-URS), enabling perception-driven autonomy such as visual servoing and stone targeting. However, acquiring and annotating large-scale surgical datasets is costly, time-consuming, and constrained by privacy and procedural variability. We present a fully synthetic dataset generation pipeline for kidney stone instance segmentation using a high-fidelity digital twin of our custom-built autonomous robotic-assisted ureteroscopy (ARA-URS) physical platform, simulated within NVIDIA Isaac Sim. We train a YOLO26 segmentation model on six datasets comprising fully synthetic data (20,000 images), hand-annotated real surgical footage (1,433 images), and mixed real–synthetic combinations, and evaluate them on a held-out set of 162 real surgical images from three unseen procedures. While the synthetic-only model achieves a Mask mAP50 of 0.818, the hybrid dataset with 5% real and 95% synthetic data outperforms both the fully real dataset (0.939 vs. 0.913) and the 1,000-image real-only dataset (0.839). Furthermore, all trained models substantially outperform a recent peer-reviewed baseline architecture (Mask mAP50: 0.440), establishing a strong comparative benchmark. The model is further evaluated in a clinically relevant benchtop task, where the predicted segmentation mask is used to estimate the three-dimensional position of the stone. An inverse kinemat- ics (IK) solver then generates joint commands to autonomously guide the physical robotic system toward the target, positioning the laser in a ready-to-fire configuration for stone ablation. The ARA-URS system successfully reached the target in 20 of 30 trials. These results demonstrate that synthetic data can serve as a scalable and effective strategy for enabling perception-driven autonomy in surgical robotic systems.

An Adaptive LQG Model of Human Learning in Surgical Robot Teleoperation
Yinqi Huang, Yilin Cai, Mengyao Li, Yue Chen, Robert C. Wilson
Abstract

Surgical digital twins require models of the adapting human operator in addition to models of the robot and scene. Twenty-four participants practiced a ring-through-rail teleoperation task on the da Vinci Research Kit while robot kinematics were recorded. An adaptive linear-quadratic-Gaussian (LQG) controller performed matched rail-following tasks. The controller combines Kalman state estimation with optimal feedback control and maintains an internal estimate of the robot motion-scaling mapping. Prediction error updates this estimate over practice. Human trajectories became more direct with training, particularly in translational movement efficiency. The adaptive LQG controller showed a corresponding reduction in translational path length as its internal mapping estimate converged toward the true mapping. The comparison is qualitative rather than an individual-level fit, but both humans and the model exhibit the same principal signature of learning: progressively more efficient translational control with practice. Adaptive optimal control therefore provides an interpretable baseline for representing the changing operator within an operator-aware surgical twin, while remaining an idealized model rather than a complete account of surgical perception or expertise.
